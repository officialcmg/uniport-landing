## How it works

When a payment completes, `onSuccess` gives your page the payment’s `depositAddress`. Send it to your server. Your server asks Uniport about that address and checks that the answer matches the order.

<Code
  file="Checkout.tsx"
  code={`<UniportButton
  appId="app_..."
  recipient="0x..."
  destinationToken="arbitrumUSDC"
  amount="25"
  onSuccess={(result) =>
    fetch("/api/orders/confirm", {
      method: "POST",
      body: JSON.stringify({ orderId, depositAddress: result.depositAddress }),
    })
  }
/>`}
/>

## Check the payment

Call `GET https://api.uniport.dev/v1/payments/{depositAddress}` from your server. No key is needed. On chains that use a memo, add `?memo=` with the `depositMemo` from `onSuccess`.

<Code
  file="confirm.ts"
  lang="ts"
  code={`const res = await fetch(
  \`https://api.uniport.dev/v1/payments/\${depositAddress}\`
);
const payment = await res.json();

const paid =
  payment.status === "SUCCESS" &&
  payment.appId === process.env.UNIPORT_APP_ID &&
  payment.recipient.toLowerCase() === MY_WALLET.toLowerCase() &&
  payment.destinationAsset === EXPECTED_ASSET &&
  BigInt(payment.amountOut) >= EXPECTED_AMOUNT &&
  !(await alreadyUsed(depositAddress));`}
/>

## What this does not do

It does not tie a payment to one particular order. The check proves that a payment of the right amount reached your address through your app, and that you have not used it before. If two customers place orders for the same amount at the same time, the only thing telling their payments apart is which deposit address each browser reports. If that matters for what you sell, give each order its own amount or its own recipient address.

<Callout tone="warn" title="Check on every order.">A page can call your confirm endpoint with any deposit address it likes. Never mark an order paid without running the checks above on your server.</Callout>
